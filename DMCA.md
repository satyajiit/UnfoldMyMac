# Notice and takedown

UnfoldMyMac is a free, open-source Mac app. Some of its designs are tributes to games,
products and brands we admire, and those tributes include material owned by other
people: logos, promotional artwork and names.

**If you hold rights in something this project ships and you want it gone, email
[admin@matterwardlabs.com](mailto:admin@matterwardlabs.com) with `TAKEDOWN` in the
subject. We aim to remove it within two hours of reading your message, and we will not
ask you to argue the point first.**

We would rather lose a wallpaper than keep one that is not welcome.

## What we need from you

Nothing notarized, nothing formal. A short email is enough:

1. What material you are asking about — a file path, a wallpaper name, a screenshot or a
   link to the page showing it. If in doubt, describe it and we will find it.
2. Who you are and who you act for.
3. Where the original lives, if it is not obvious.
4. An address we can reply to.

If you prefer a formal DMCA notice under 17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3), send that instead and it
will be honoured the same way. You can also report it to GitHub directly through
[GitHub's DMCA process](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/content-removal-policies/dmca-takedown-policy),
though writing to us is faster.

## What we do when you write

1. **Remove the material** from the `main` branch, usually in a single commit, and reply
   telling you the commit that did it.
2. **Publish a new release** with the material gone. Anyone on 1.0.2 or later receives it
   through the in-app updater without doing anything, usually within a day.
3. **Withdraw the affected release assets** if you ask us to. The previous disk images
   stay downloadable from GitHub unless you want them pulled too — say the word and they
   go.
4. **Leave it removed.** We do not reinstate material after a takedown unless you tell us
   in writing that we may.

Git history is not rewritten by default, because force-pushing a public repository breaks
every clone and fork of it. If you need the material expunged from history as well, say so
and we will do it.

## What we ship, and what we claim

Being precise about this matters more than a disclaimer does, so the exact provenance of
every borrowed asset is recorded in [NOTICE](NOTICE) and, for the game collection, in
[UnfoldMyMac/GAME_WALLPAPERS.md](UnfoldMyMac/GAME_WALLPAPERS.md) — including the source
URL each file came from and the date it was retrieved.

In short, for the ten game wallpapers:

| What | Where it came from |
| --- | --- |
| The game logo | The publisher's own logo, downloaded from Steam's CDN or PlayStation, **unmodified** |
| The detail-page banner | The publisher's official promotional artwork, converted from JPEG to PNG |
| The animated background | AI-generated fan art, upscaled locally. Not the publisher's artwork |
| The scene itself | Original Metal shaders written for this project |

**All logos, promotional artwork, game names and character names belong to their owners.**
They are excluded from this project's Apache 2.0 licence. Nothing in that licence, in this
file, or in `NOTICE` grants anyone any right to them.

UnfoldMyMac is **not affiliated with, sponsored by, endorsed by or connected to** any of
the publishers, studios or rightsholders named anywhere in this project. Every game
wallpaper is an independent tribute made by a fan.

## Why we think this is reasonable

We include these tributes because they show what the wallpaper engine can do with live
system data, and because people like seeing a game they love react to their Mac's battery
or network. The app is **free**, **open source** and **not monetised** — there is no
purchase, no subscription, no advertising and no telemetry. Nothing here is sold, and no
part of the project competes with the games it references.

We believe that is a fair and non-commercial use, and in many places it is also
recognised as nominative use — you cannot refer to Elden Ring without saying "Elden Ring".

But we want to be straight about the limits of that reasoning rather than hide behind it:
a good-faith belief is not a licence, and "educational" or "promotional" is one factor a
court weighs, not a switch that settles the question. This policy exists precisely because
we would rather be easy to correct than be right.
